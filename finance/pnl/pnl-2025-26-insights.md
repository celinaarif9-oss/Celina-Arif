**Headline:** product economics are healthy — 48% gross margin, in line with the per-article
costing. But **net margin is only 6.6%** — operating costs eat almost all the gross profit.

## Marketing is your #1 cost — Rs4.44M (13.3% of sales)
"Add charges" is **advertising**, so the true marketing spend is:

| | PKR | % of sales |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing — Pitch (8% + retainer) | 3,084,031 | 9.2% |
| Advertising (ad spend) | 1,353,929 | 4.0% |
| **Total marketing** | **4,437,960** | **13.3%** |

That's the **single biggest operating cost** — bigger than courier. On a 6.6% net margin, marketing
is spending ~2× your entire net profit. **The key question is what it returns:** at 13.3% of sales
you'd want to see the sales it drives (ROAS). That's exactly what the marketing/KPI dashboard would
show — I'd make that the next priority.

## Courier — Rs3.64M (10.9%), but mostly recovered from customers
Courier looks alarming, but it's largely **pass-through**, not a leak:

- The one month we can cross-check is **May**: courier cost **661,200** vs shipping charged to
  customers **603,336** → you absorbed only **~57,864 (recovered 91%)**.
- If that ~90% recovery holds across the year, the *real* absorbed courier cost is only
  **~Rs350–400k**, not Rs3.6M. Customers are paying most of it.

## Seasonality — half the months ran at a loss
Feb carried the year (Rs8.4M sales, Rs2.98M net, 35%). Low-sales months lose money because fixed
costs (rent + salaries ≈ Rs1M/month) run regardless. Six of twelve months were negative.

## Where to focus (in order)
1. **Marketing (13.3%)** — measure the return; it's the biggest and most controllable cost.
2. **Smooth the quiet months** — they turn the year's profit negative.
3. **Courier recovery** — mostly fine, but tighten the ~10% you absorb (send Shopify shipping to confirm).

## Note
Production **staff salaries** are treated as cost of goods here. If your accountant prefers them as
an operating expense, net profit is identical — only the gross/operating split shifts.
