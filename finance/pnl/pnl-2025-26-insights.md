# FY 2025–26 P&L — what the numbers show

Built from the accountant's income statement (Jul 2025 – Jun 2026). Management view — **before
tax**; your accountant confirms filings. The full sheet is `pnl-2025-26-actuals.csv`.

## The year at a glance
| | PKR | % of sales |
|---|---|---|
| **Sales** | 33,502,078 | 100% |
| Cost of goods (cloth/dye + production salaries) | 17,275,261 | 51.6% |
| **Gross profit** | **16,226,817** | **48.4%** |
| Operating expenses | 14,030,213 | 41.9% |
| **Net profit (before tax)** | **2,196,604** | **6.6%** |

**The headline:** your *product economics are healthy* — 48% gross margin, in line with the
per-article costing. But **net margin is thin at 6.6%**, because operating costs eat almost all of
the gross profit. The money is made in the making and lost in the running.

**Two levers stand out:**
- **Courier at ~11% of sales** is the single biggest operating cost — this lines up with the
  earlier shipping finding that you're absorbing delivery. Recovering more shipping from customers
  and/or negotiating courier rates is the highest-impact fix on this whole page.
- **Marketing (Pitch) at ~9%** — the 8% commission + retainer. Worth watching against the ROAS it
  delivers once the KPI dashboard is live.
- **"Add charges" 1.35M** — a large, unlabelled line. Worth asking the accountant what it covers.

**Feb carried the whole year** (2.98M net — likely peak/wedding season). Low-sales months lose
money because fixed costs (rent + salaries ≈ Rs1M/month) run regardless. The path to a healthier
net margin is (1) smoothing the quiet months and (2) getting courier + marketing under control.

## Note
Production **staff salaries** are treated as cost of goods here (they're the making cost). If your
accountant prefers them as an operating expense, the net profit is identical — only the gross/
operating split shifts.
